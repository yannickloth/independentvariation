IVP Development Topics — Complete Inventory

Purpose: Exhaustive inventory of every topic, theory, and development thread related to the Independent Variation Principle (IVP) and its broader theory, as present in this project (src/, meta/) and in the literature submodules (literature/PIV, literature/PIV-Article, literature/PIV-Formalization-ICSA2026).

Use: This list drives the navigation structure of the static IVP web site. Each topic is a candidate page, section, or category. The list is complete as of 2026-08-27.

Part 0 — The Foundational Postulate: CMH

The Change Management Hypothesis (CMH) is the normative postulate that structure is optimal when it minimizes lifetime coordination cost around change drivers. IVP is derived from CMH.

  • CMH as the surviving foundation among competing modularization-theory alternatives (survey of six alternatives).
  • CMH applied to enterprise-architecture structuring (nine alternative foundations evaluated; CMH unique).
  • Graph-theoretic proof that CMH prescribes exactly four cost-optimal conditions (bipartite incidence graphs, gamma-equality).
  • CMH as a normative postulate, not an empirical hypothesis (historical classification).
  • The two-stage adoption structure of CMH (motivational introduction → formal development).
  • Volume-0: “The Change Management Hypothesis: Foundations of Modularization Theory” — full CMH treatment across software, organizations, business processes.
  • Business formulation of CMH: structure is optimal when it minimizes lifetime coordination cost around change drivers (enterprise/business application).
  • CMH derivation chain: system tuple, cost factorization, model-fidelity premise, structural exhaustion (M1–M4), CMH-compatible cost-model class, four conditions.
  • Brooks’s Law derived as a corollary of CMH.
  • The coordination-cost derivation of IVP from CMH (the “coordination anchor”).

Part 1 — The Core Theory of IVP

The formal apparatus of IVP: system tuple, change drivers, elements, modules, knowledge, coupling, and the four axioms.

  • Software system tuple S = (F, κ_F, E, 𝒦, C, Γ) — functional purpose, relevant causal domain knowledge, elements, coupling relation, change-driver set, assignment.
  • Knowledge structure — Knowledge, Domain Knowledge, Causal Domain Knowledge, Relevant Causal Domain Knowledge, Knowledge Slices (κ_F = ⊔ᵢ κ_γᵢ).
  • Change drivers γ ∈ C — discrete sources of variation; coupling dimension = leaf-level driver.
  • Change-driver assignment Γ: E → 𝒫(C); change-driver independence γᵢ ⊥ γⱼ; causal dependence between drivers γⱼ ⇝ γᵢ.
  • Change-driver refinement γ ▷ {γ₁,…,γₙ} — domain decomposition.
  • Elements e ∈ E — pure, composite, irreducible-composite (translation/adapter, combination), reducible-composite.
  • Modules M ⊆ E and Modularization ℳ (partition of E); module driver set Γ(M) = ⋃_{e∈M} Γ(e).
  • Domain — coherent region of causal knowledge; more precise than DDD’s “domain.”
  • Variation dependence — Γ-equivalence classes; the equivalence relation forming the IVP-compliant partition.
  • The four IVP axioms — IVP-1 (Element Admissibility), IVP-2 (Change-Driver Assignment), IVP-3 (Unit Purity/Separation), IVP-4 (Unit Completeness/Unification); the biconditional e₁~e₂ ⟺ Γ(e₁)=Γ(e₂).
  • Partition Theorem — IVP-compliance gives a unique (up to coarsening) optimal partition.
  • Knowledge Theorem — cohesion ⟺ knowledge embodiment; maximal cohesion (purity+completeness) ⟺ complete+pure formalization of domain knowledge.
  • Knowledge embodiment / pure knowledge / complete knowledge / maximal knowledge embodiment.
  • Causal cohesion — Γ-equivalence as modularization principle; causal cohesion metric (purity, completeness).
  • Coupling — the coupling relation 𝒦, coupling instances, propagation mechanisms (structural/temporal/concurrency/semantic/platform), coupling dimensions.
  • Coupling decomposition — necessary + spurious + accidental; structural vs causal dependencies.
  • Causal coupling / cohesion primacy — coupling is a consequence of cohesion, not an independent trade-off.
  • The six-step IVP application process — pre-design, design, validation.
  • Driver omission theory — omitting a driver coarsens the optimal partition; remediation cost; orthogonality vs entanglement; triage.
  • Refactoring convergence theorem — change-driver-directed moves strictly decrease distance to the Γ-optimal partition.
  • Multi-dimensional coupling — six coupling dimensions; Γ-partition eliminates accidental coupling in all at once.
  • Objects as modules — IVP criterion for what makes a correct object at object granularity.
  • Domain–infrastructure boundary — as a structural property of the driver set.
  • Acyclicity / dependency cycles — three graphs, types of knowledge cycles, breaking fundamental cycles.

Pointed results about individual principles, mostly negative or corrective. These are the “SRP is false”-style results.

  • SRP is false — SRP as Martin states it fails; the adapter pattern is a counterexample; cardinality + people-grounding fail.
  • SRP vocabulary problem — why three decades of reformulations failed (vocabulary hid the cardinality error).
  • SRP–CCP–ISP triangle — the three collapse into one partitioning mechanism.
  • CCP is not a principle — “change together” admits two readings, only one survives.
  • ISP is DIP — ISP is a conditional corollary of DIP applied per client; gated by interface-evolution origin.
  • ISP is DIP (packaging) — where client-owned interfaces physically live.
  • ISP is DIP (with IVP) — formal IVP proof.
  • DIP is a theorem of IVP — from independent variation, decisional dependency, contract-feasibility.
  • DIP domain knowledge — interfaces embody domain knowledge vs. form overhead.
  • SDP causal stability — Stable Dependencies derived causally (vs. Martin’s metric-only stability).
  • What remains of SOLID — deconstruction; what conditionally survives (LSP/OCP dismissed).
  • SRP formalization (shared definitions).
  • Martin heuristics note — SOLID/package rules are informal heuristics, not formal principles.
  • GRASP deconstructed — nine patterns reduce to an unresolved Low-Coupling/High-Cohesion tension with no decision procedure.
  • Co-change impossibility — co-change (MSR) cannot recover any partitioning rule; abductive candidate generation only.
  • Co-change driver structure — the impossibility instantiated for Γ.
  • Co-change practitioner guide — the surviving abductive role.
